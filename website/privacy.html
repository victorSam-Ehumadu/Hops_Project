Privacy

Privacy Policy

Hops is built so that almost nothing about you leaves your phone. This page says exactly what the short list of exceptions is, who holds it, and how to get it back or have it deleted.

Last updated 5 November 2026.

The short version

We store your username, your email address, and a salted hash of your password. That is the whole list.

Your messages, contacts, tasks, study tags, vibe reports, game progress and files are stored only on your own device. They are never uploaded, so there is no copy of them for us, for your campus admins, or for anyone else to read, hand over or lose.

We do not sell your information, we do not share it with advertisers, and Hops contains no third-party analytics or advertising code.

What we hold, and why

Username

So that other people can add you, and so that moderators can act on a report. It is the name other users see.

Email address

Used only to verify your account, reset a forgotten password, and confirm changes to your account. It is encrypted at rest, and we never send marketing to it.

Salted password hash

Your password itself is never sent to us. It is combined with a random value unique to your account and hashed on your phone, and only the result is stored. Nobody at Hops can read or recover your password.

A public key

Generated on your phone so that emergency alerts can be proved genuine. The matching private key stays in your device's secure storage and is never sent anywhere.

Moderation records

If you are reported, warned, suspended or banned, that decision and its reason are recorded so the campus team can review it and so it can be reversed.

Community Alerts

Safety announcements written by your campus admins. These are broadcast to everyone by design, and are the only messages that pass through a server.

What never leaves your phone

These exist only in Hops' local storage on your device. There is no server-side copy, which also means we cannot recover them for you if you lose the device without taking a backup first.

  • One-to-one chat messages, and anything you say to the built-in assistant
  • Your contacts, their profile pictures and their bios
  • Tasks and reminders
  • Vibe Check reports, Study Bunny tags and Lost & Found posts
  • Carrot balance, cosmetics, game scores and streaks
  • Files sent or received through Hops Share

No location data, ever. Hops contains no GPS or location-tracking code of any kind. Android asks for a location permission because the operating system requires it before any app is allowed to scan for Bluetooth devices — not because Hops wants your position. We never read it, store it or send it. When you pick a place in Study Bunny you are choosing from a fixed list of campus venues, which is a label, not a coordinate.

What nearby devices can see

Hops works by sending very short messages over Bluetooth. Two things are worth knowing about what that looks like from the outside.

When your phone is idle

It broadcasts a rotating identifier — a short code that changes periodically and contains no part of your name. It cannot be used to identify you or follow your movements, and only people you have already chatted with can recognise it as yours.

While your phone is sending

Hops has to put its messages inside the Bluetooth name your phone advertises, because that is the only field available to it. So for the few seconds a message is being sent, your phone's Bluetooth name is temporarily set to that message, and any nearby Bluetooth device may display it — including devices without Hops installed. Some messages contain the first few characters of your username, such as a friend request or an emergency alert. Hops restores your real Bluetooth name automatically afterwards, and remembers it so it can still be restored if the app is closed mid-send.

Your phone helps carry emergency alerts

So that an alert can travel further than a single phone's range, every device running Hops automatically re-sends any emergency alert or Lost & Found post it hears, once each, for a few seconds. This is what lets an alert cross a building. It applies only to those broadcast alerts — your private messages are never relayed through anyone else's device — and a phone without Hops installed never relays anything.

Your rights, and how to use them

Under the Protection of Personal Information Act (POPIA) in South Africa — and under the GDPR if you are in the UK or EU — you have the right to see what we hold about you, to correct it, and to have it deleted. Hops implements these in the app itself, so you do not have to ask anyone's permission or wait for a reply.

See and take a copy of your data

Profile → Account Management → Export My Data. This writes everything Hops holds on your device to a single file you choose the location of. It works offline.

Correct it

Profile → Edit Identity for your display details, and Account Management to change your email address or password.

Delete it

Profile → Account Management → Delete Account. This removes your account from our servers and erases Hops' data from the device. Hops offers to export your data first, because the deletion cannot be undone and there is no server copy to restore from.

Object, or ask a human

Use the contact details below. If you are not satisfied with our response you may complain to the Information Regulator of South Africa.

Being banned does not delete your data. If a moderator suspends or bans your account you lose access to the network, but your messages, contacts and tasks stay on your phone untouched — because a ban can be lifted, and losing your own data should not be a side effect of a decision that might be reversed.

How long we keep things

  • Your account — for as long as it exists. Delete it and the record is removed.
  • Sign-in codes sent by email — these expire within minutes, and we never store a readable copy of one.
  • Moderation records — kept while the account exists, so that a decision can be reviewed or reversed. Removed with the account.
  • Community Alerts — kept until your campus admin team deletes them.
  • Everything on your device — until you delete it, uninstall Hops, or sign out. Signing out clears Hops' local data, which is why Hops offers you an export first.

Who else is involved

Hops uses two service providers, and only for the account functions described above: a hosted database provider that stores the account record, and an email delivery provider that sends verification and password-reset messages. Neither receives any of your on-device content, because none of it is ever uploaded. Hops contains no advertising or analytics services.

Children

Hops is intended for users aged 13 and over, in line with our Terms. We do not knowingly collect information from anyone younger.

Changes to this policy

If we change what we collect or why, we will update this page and change the date at the top. Material changes will also be surfaced in the app.
